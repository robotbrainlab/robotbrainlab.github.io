# Brief — fix verification (step 5 of the chapter loop)

**A different agent from the one that did step 3.** Fill this in and give it to them. Do
not rewrite it. Replace every `<…>`.

---

## Why this pass exists

The recurring defect is not writing a chapter badly. **It is fixing a claim where you are
looking and missing it where you are not.** In the project this loop came from, this pass
caught between four and thirteen survivors per chapter, every chapter, with no exception.

## The task

**Chapter:** `<path>`

**Read ONLY these passages. They are the ones that changed.**

`<list every changed passage: section name and line range, one per line. An agent told
only "look at the chapter" re-reads everything and finds what step 3 already found.>`

**The findings these changes were meant to fix:**

`<paste each finding from step 3, and the fix that was applied for it>`

## What to check, in this order

1. **Did the fix land?** For each finding, does the changed passage now say the right
   thing?
2. **Did the fix break the sentence around it?** A corrected clause in a paragraph written
   for the old claim is a new defect.
3. **Did the fix survive everywhere?** Search the whole chapter for every phrasing of the
   old claim, not only the one that was edited. **Report the count you found.**
   **Look in the back matter first.** The survivor is usually in a Summary, a Key Terms
   row or a Review Answer — sections read as settled because they are a glossary or a crib
   rather than an argument. That is where a fix goes to not happen.
4. **If the chapter has a lab**, one corrected claim lives in **six** places, and four are
   prose: the program's `print()` lines, that program's captured output, the body, the
   Summary, the Worth Memorising table, and the Review Answers. **The captured output is
   the one that gets missed**, because a corrected program is not a corrected chapter
   until its output has been produced again.
5. **Did the fix round introduce anything new?** A fix round that touched more than a
   handful of passages is a draft, not an edit. Treat it as one.

## How to answer

- One line per changed passage: landed · did not land · landed and broke something.
- Then the survivors, with line numbers and the count.
- **If unsure, say so rather than asserting.**

## Stopping rule

Stop when every listed passage has a verdict and every search has a count. Do not open
another chapter.
