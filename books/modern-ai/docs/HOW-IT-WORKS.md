# How this book gets made, and when I need you

**Written for the owner, 2026-11-12, at his request.** He asked for the steps in plain language so
he does not have to ask again.

**This file states no rules. It is a map.** Each step names the file that owns it. Where this file
and that file disagree, **that file wins** and this one is out of date.

---

## The short answer

**You do four things. I do everything else.**

You decide the areas. You decide the subjects. You decide the book's shape. You approve the
contents. After that the writing runs without you, and you read the chapters.

**You never write a proposal, a list or a brief.** I write them. You approve, change or reject.

---

## Part 1 — Getting to chapter one. Eleven steps, once

| # | Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agree the goal | **You** | Why the book exists. **Done** |
| 2 | Agree the method | **You** | How it gets built. **Partly done**, and the rest is settled as it comes up |
| 3 | Write the research brief | Me | The instructions a research run follows. **Done** |
| 4 | **Establish the areas** | Me, then **you** | Two or more runs record how the field files its own work. A third run merges them into one list. **I propose which areas the book covers, with a reason for each.** You approve, redraw or reject. **This is where we are now** |
| 5 | Run the research | Me | One research run per approved area. It returns subjects and claims |
| 6 | **Select the subjects** | Me, then **you** | I sort every subject against your three tests. You see what passed and every rejection with the reason. **You rule on both** |
| 7 | **Decide the shape** | **You** | Four questions that need the subjects first: breadth against depth, how deep the mathematics goes, the structure, and which area goes deep |
| 8 | **Approve the contents** | **You** | The chapter list |
| 9 | Write the acceptance questions | Me | The questions each chapter must answer, written **before** any chapter |
| 10 | Build the machinery | Me | The checks that read each finished chapter |
| 11 | Write chapter one | Me | Then you read it, run its lab and answer its questions. **Both durations are measured**, and the next round is sized from them |

## Part 2 — After chapter one. A repeating cycle

Eight stages, in this order, over and over.

| Stage | What happens | You? |
|---|---|---|
| **Expire** | Every claim past its review date is checked again. Some are confirmed, some are disputed, some are out of date | **For the load-bearing ones.** You also re-answer earlier chapters' questions here |
| **Research** | One run per area. It returns subjects and claims | No |
| **Confirm** | Every claim becomes a row in a table, with its source, its strength and a quotation. Then the weak ones are checked at source | No |
| **Select** | Every subject is sorted: a chapter, a short entry, or rejected | **Yes.** You rule |
| **Plan** | Subjects become chapters. Each chapter is given what it must contain | **Yes.** You approve |
| **Write** | One chapter. Never more than one at a time | No |
| **Verify** | Four reviews read the chapter: one against its sources, one against the claims, one runs the lab from the text alone, one re-checks the fixes | No |
| **Close** | The record is sealed. The ledgers are updated. Superseded documents are moved out of the way | No |

**Expire is first on purpose.** Put it last and the cycle eats itself: it would ask for stale
claims to be re-checked before the stage that re-checks them.

## Part 3 — Where I need you, and what it costs you

**Five points per round.** Everything else runs without you.

| When | What you get | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **The areas** | A proposed list. One row per decision, each with its own reason. Every cut names what it removes | Approve it, change it, or reject it |
| **The subjects** | What passed, and every rejection with the test it failed and the research's own words for why | Rule on both |
| **The shape** | The four questions that could not be answered before the subjects existed | Decide |
| **The contents** | The chapter list | Approve |
| **A structural change** | A chapter splitting, merging, retiring, or the areas being redrawn | Approve |

**Two rules protect you at every one of them.** You are never handed a list nobody has
recommended — somebody proposes it first, with a reason for each item, and that somebody is me.
And nothing is cut silently: a stage that reduces its input must account for everything it was
given, and name what each removal takes with it.

**If you do not answer within seven days**, I act on my recommendation and mark it as mine, so the
work does not stop. **Five things never work that way**, whatever the wait: publishing, spending
your money, discarding finished work, anything you reserved, and changing anything you yourself
decided. `PROCESS.md` §2.1 owns that list.

## Part 4 — Where you are not needed, deliberately

- **Writing anything.** Chapters, briefs, proposals, lists, checks.
- **Checking the work.** Every chapter is read by four separate reviews and by a set of automatic
  checks before you see it.
- **Deciding what is true.** A claim carries its source, its strength and a quotation, and a
  separate run re-checks the weak ones at source.
- **Watching the research.** It writes to files. You read the result, not the run.
