# Frame run 2026-11-11-h - how modern AI files its own work

## The question I was asked

Under what categories does **modern artificial intelligence** currently file, review and present its own work — and what does each category include and exclude?

## Sources used

- NeurIPS 2025 Call for Papers (main track topic list) - https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/CallForPapers - read 2026-11-11 - review-venue - FETCHED
- ICML 2026 Call for Papers (topics of interest) - https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers - read 2026-11-11 - review-venue - FETCHED
- ACL Rolling Review Call for Papers (track list) - https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp - read 2026-11-11 - review-venue - FETCHED

- ACM Computing Classification System 2012, ACM's own SKOS/RDF file - https://dl.acm.org/pb-assets/dl_ccs/acm_ccs2012-1626988337597.xml - read 2026-11-11 - index - FETCHED

- ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 committee structure (working groups) - https://committee.iso.org/committee/6794475.html - read 2026-11-11 - practitioner - FETCHED

Why each was chosen:
- NeurIPS 2025 Call for Papers: the submission topic list of the largest general machine-learning venue; it is the list authors actually pick from, and it is published openly. Chosen as the anchor `review-venue`.
- ICML 2026 Call for Papers: a second general venue with a near-identical list, chosen so that one venue's wording could be checked against another's rather than taken as the field's.
- ACL Rolling Review Call for Papers: chosen because the two general venues compress all of language into one topic, and ARR is the refereeing system that splits it; it is also the only review venue I found whose track list is this fine-grained.
- arXiv category taxonomy: the archive nearly all of this work is posted to before review, so its categories are the filing step that comes first. Chosen as an `index`.
- ACM Computing Classification System 2012: a second `index`, and the only one I found that is a formal poly-hierarchy maintained by a professional body; chosen because it draws the AI/ML boundary in a place no other source does.
- AI Engineer World's Fair 2026 programme: chosen as the `programme`, and specifically an engineering meeting rather than an academic one, because the academic sources have no submission category for most deployment work.
- AAAI 2025 Presidential Panel on the Future of AI Research: chosen as the `survey` - 17 standing chapters about the research itself, written by a panel rather than one pair of hands, and not a measurement of AI's effect on the world.
- MLCommons Benchmark Suites: a vendor-neutral benchmark consortium's own divisions, named in the brief as a qualifying `practitioner` source; chosen because it carries operational categories (storage, client, edge) that no academic source files.
- ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 committee structure: a standards body's own classification of AI work, chosen as a second `practitioner` source because it carries conformity assessment, data and use cases as first-class divisions.

Source floor: **met**. 9 sources across 5 kinds - review-venue (3), index (2), programme (1), survey (1), practitioner (2). No source I used sells a product in this subject, so none is marked `vendor`.

## Sources I looked for and could not use

- **ACM Digital Library CCS browser** (https://dl.acm.org/ccs) - HTTP 403 to an automated fetch. Route that worked: ACM publishes the same classification as a SKOS/RDF file at https://dl.acm.org/pb-assets/dl_ccs/acm_ccs2012-1626988337597.xml, which returned 200 and is the source I read. I also tried https://www.acm.org/publications/class-2012 (403) and a Wayback mirror of dl.acm.org/ccs (TLS handshake failure).
- **ISO's own committee page** (https://www.iso.org/committee/6794475.html) - HTTP 403. Route that worked: the same committee page on ISO's committee host, https://committee.iso.org/committee/6794475.html, returned 200 with the full structure table. A JTC1 mirror (https://jtc1info.org/jtc1-subcommittees/sc-42/) returned a truncated page and a UNT web-archive copy returned a 5 KB stub.
- **AAAI's own page for the Presidential Panel report** (https://aaai.org/about-aaai/presidential-panel-on-the-future-of-ai-research/) - it states the report has 17 chapters but does not list them. The report PDF (https://iagen.unam.mx/recursos/AAAI-2025-PresPanel-Report-FINAL.pdf) exceeded the fetch size limit. Route that worked: a faithful reproduction of AAAI's own chapter list at https://robohub.org/report-on-the-future-of-ai-research/, which introduces it as "The 17 topics, each with a dedicated chapter, are as follows." The classifying body is AAAI; Robohub is the route. I could not open the chapters themselves, so each AAAI unit's INCLUDES is an admission, not a quotation.
- **AITopics** (https://aitopics.org/search), AAAI's AI information portal - I expected a browsable subject taxonomy. What the page exposes is a filter panel (Technology, Industry, Genre, Theme, Conference, Country, Journal, Publisher, Source) whose Technology facet showed only one top node to the fetch. Not a readable filing system at the level I needed; dropped.
- **aijobs.net** (https://aijobs.net/) - wanted as a named job board's categories, which would have carried roles and occupations. The page returned 200 but its navigation is script-rendered and the HTML carried no category or tag links. Dropped. This is the main hole in my practitioner coverage: I have no source that files AI work by job or role.
- **AAAI-26 main track area list** - I searched for it but the search returned only the AI for Social Impact track call and other special-track calls, not the main track's own area list. Not fetched, so AAAI's review-venue categories are absent from this run; only its survey-kind report is here.

## Disagreements

Reported, not resolved.

1. **Where machine learning sits relative to artificial intelligence.** The ACM CCS makes `Machine learning` a *sibling* of `Artificial intelligence` under Computing methodologies. arXiv makes `cs.LG` a peer of `cs.AI` and defines cs.AI by subtraction: "Covers all areas of AI except Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, and Computation and Language". NeurIPS and ICML have no category called artificial intelligence at all - machine learning is the frame of the whole call, and everything else is a topic inside it. Three sources, three different containments.

2. **Where vision, language and robotics go.** The ACM CCS files `Computer vision` and `Natural language processing` *inside* Artificial intelligence (one level below the level I took). arXiv makes them peer categories (`cs.CV`, `cs.CL`, `cs.RO`). NeurIPS compresses all of them into one topic, "Applications (e.g., vision, language, speech and audio, Creative AI)". ACL Rolling Review splits language alone into 31 tracks. The same work is one category, three categories, part of one category, or thirty-one, depending on who is filing it.

3. **Trustworthiness, safety, ethics and fairness.** ICML binds them into one topic, `trustworthy machine learning`, framed as reliability. NeurIPS files the same words under `Social and economic aspects of machine learning`, framed as social and economic. ARR files `Ethics, Bias, and Fairness` and, separately, `Interpretability and Analysis of Models for NLP`. The AAAI panel report splits them across two chapters, `AI Factuality & Trustworthiness` and `AI Ethics & Safety`. SC 42 has `Trustworthiness` (WG 3) and *also* a separate functional-safety joint working group (JWG 4) and a conformity-assessment one (JWG 6). Five sources, five cuts.

4. **Causality.** NeurIPS names causal inference inside `Probabilistic methods`. ICML names causality inside `trustworthy machine learning`. The two calls are otherwise near-identical lists.

5. **Hardware.** ICML names hardware inside `machine learning systems`. NeurIPS's `Infrastructure` parenthetical does not name it. The AAAI panel report gives it a chapter of its own, `Hardware & AI`. arXiv gives it `cs.AR`. MLCommons does not file hardware as a category at all - it is the thing every MLPerf suite measures.

6. **Evaluation has six different homes.** `Evaluation` (NeurIPS, ICML, a methodology topic); `Resources and Evaluation` (ARR, bundled with datasets); `AI Evaluation` (AAAI, a research chapter); `Evals` (AI Engineer World's Fair, an engineering track); twelve whole benchmark suites (MLCommons); and two SC 42 joint working groups - testing of AI-based systems with SC 7 (JWG 2) and conformity assessment with CASCO (JWG 6). No two of these draw the same boundary.

7. **Agents.** ARR scopes the category to language, `LLM Agents for NLP`. arXiv has `cs.MA (Multiagent Systems)`, which predates the current usage and is defined as "multiagent systems, distributed artificial intelligence, intelligent agents". The AAAI panel report has one chapter, `AI Agents`. The AI Engineer World's Fair splits the same ground across four labels - `Claws & Personal Agents`, `Agentic Engineering`, `Computer Use` and `Agentic Commerce`.

8. **Robotics and embodiment.** arXiv has `cs.RO (Robotics)`. The AAAI panel report calls the chapter `Embodied AI`. NeurIPS and ICML have no robotics topic and name robotics inside the `Reinforcement learning` parenthetical. The AI Engineer World's Fair calls the track `Robotics & World Models`.

9. **Two arXiv categories are aliases of categories in other archives.** The source states that `cs.NA` is an alias for math.NA and `cs.SY` is an alias for eess.SY - the same category is filed under two archives at once, which no other source in this run does.

10. **Whether the labels at a level are subjects at all.** At the level I took, the AI Engineer World's Fair programme mixes subject tracks with a keynote slot, two workshop days, a networking room and three audience groupings by job role (`AI-Native Enterprises`, `AI Architects: ...`, `CTO Circle`). SC 42's structure level mixes subject working groups with a liaison group (`Liaison with SC 27`) and a procedural one (`Best practices for new proposals`). Every academic source's level holds subjects only. I have reported all of them as units with their nature stated, as instructed; deciding what to do about that is not this stage's job.

11. **Artificial general intelligence.** The AAAI panel report gives `Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)` a standing chapter. No review venue, index, programme or practitioner source I read carries a category of that name. The same is true of four more of its chapters - `AI Perception vs. Reality`, `Diversity of AI Research Approaches`, `Research Beyond the AI Research Community`, `Role of Academia` and `Geopolitical Aspects & Implications of AI`.

12. **A source disagreeing with itself.** The MLCommons page gives `MLPerf Endpoints` and `MLPerf Inference: Datacenter` the same scope sentence word for word - "Measures how fast systems can process inputs and produce results". The source's own text does not distinguish the two suites.
