# Candidate areas — how modern AI files its own work (run 2026-11-11-i)

## The question I was asked

Under what categories does **modern artificial intelligence** currently file, review and present its own work — and what does each category include and exclude?

You are describing how this subject is already organised, **by the people who work in it**. You are not proposing how it should be organised, and you are not saying which parts matter.

## Disagreements

Reported, not resolved.

1. **Is machine learning inside AI?** S6 (ACM CCS) files "Machine learning" as a **sibling** of "Artificial intelligence", both children of "Computing methodologies" — so in that index, machine learning is not a kind of AI. S5 (arXiv) files cs.LG as a sibling of cs.AI and says cs.AI "Covers all areas of AI except ... Machine Learning". S3 (AAAI-26) files Machine Learning as one of 16 areas **inside** an AI conference, and it is the largest of them.
2. **Is "Artificial Intelligence" a parent or a remainder?** S6 gives it 8 children. S5 defines cs.AI by subtraction: everything left after Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems and Computation and Language are removed. One source treats the name as a genus, the other as a residue.
3. **Where does probabilistic reasoning go?** S6 files "Probabilistic reasoning" inside Knowledge representation and reasoning. S3 pulls it out into a separate area, Reasoning under Uncertainty, and leaves KRR symbolic.
4. **Where does causality go?** S1 names "causal inference" inside Probabilistic methods. S2 names "causality" inside Trustworthy Machine Learning. Two venues of the same kind, in the same year, put the same word in different boxes.
5. **Does the theory topic include control theory?** S1's Theory examples name control theory; S2's Theory of Machine Learning examples do not, naming bandits and decision theory instead.
6. **What is an "application"?** S1 fills Applications with modalities (vision, language, speech and audio, Creative AI) and puts the sciences in a second topic. S2 fills Application-Driven Machine Learning with sectors and has no modality topic. S3 fills Application Domains with sectors but would route a vision paper to its Computer Vision area. S10 names exactly three application domains.
7. **One trustworthiness box or five?** S2 has one topic naming causality, fairness, interpretability, privacy, robustness and safety. S10 splits the same material across Security and privacy, Robustness to distribution shifts, AI safety and alignment, Interpretability and Inequity and fairness. S1 renames the cluster after its social and economic character. S8 makes it a standards working group, WG 3.
8. **What does "search" mean?** At S6 and S3 it is state-space search (game tree search, heuristic search). At S9 it is retrieval (Retrieval + Search, "embedding-based retrieval from internal data"). The two senses coexist at the same level in different sources.
9. **Where does speech go?** S6 files "Speech recognition" inside Natural language processing. S5 has a separate cs.SD (Sound) that "intersects with ... I.2.7". S9 gives Voice a product track. S1 names "speech and audio" inside Applications.
10. **Is robotics a category of AI at all?** S5 and S10 give Robotics a box. S3 gives Intelligent Robots an area. S6 gives it **no** child under Artificial intelligence: robotic planning appears inside two other children and cognitive robotics inside a third.
11. **Infrastructure, systems, or neither?** Four names for one region: "Infrastructure" (S1), "Machine Learning Systems" (S2), "Systems" (S10), "AI Infrastructure" (S9). S3 has no area for it and S5 has no ML-systems category. Kept as four units, because no two labels are the same.
12. **Is planning its own area?** S6 and S3 give planning a top-level box; S1 and S2 name planning only as an example inside Reinforcement Learning; S5 names it only inside the cs.AI description.
13. **Does measurement divide by method or by deployment?** S1, S2 and S10 have one Evaluation category each. S7 divides measurement into twelve suites by deployment class and by what is varied. S9 splits it into Evals and Agent Reliability.
14. **What is SC 42's WG 2 called?** The route reads "Big Data"; an earlier ISO committee listing read "Data". Similarly WG 5 appears as "Computational approaches and characteristics of artificial intelligence systems" on the route and "Computational approaches and computational characteristics of AI systems" in a 2024 plenary agenda.
15. **Two MLCommons suites share one scope sentence.** "MLPerf Inference: Datacenter" and "MLPerf Endpoints" carry word-for-word the same description on the page I read. I did not open the suites' own pages, so I cannot say how the body distinguishes them.

**How I chose my sources.** I started from the question's own distinction between filing for review, filing for storage, filing for a meeting, filing for exposition and filing for work, and looked for one or two sources of each kind that publish their categories as a list rather than as prose. Inside `review-venue` I deliberately took two machine-learning venues and one general-AI venue, so that a disagreement between venues of the same kind would be visible rather than averaged away; the AAAI keywords page was preferred over AAAI's prose scope sentence because it is the list authors actually choose from. Inside `index` I wanted one archive that authors file into themselves (arXiv) and one classification imposed by a publisher (ACM CCS), because those two pressures produce different trees — and ACM CCS was the only index I found that subdivides "Artificial intelligence" at all. For `practitioner` I took the benchmark consortium named in the brief and a standards body, because between them they carry the deployment and conformance categories no academic source has a box for. For `programme` I took a practitioner meeting rather than an academic one, on the reasoning that an academic conference's sessions mostly restate its own CFP and would have added a fourth near-copy of the same list. The `survey` slot is the weakest: I searched twice for a field-wide survey newer than 2021 and did not find one that was both field-wide and reachable, so I fell back to a 2021 report whose standing sections are at least multi-author and explicitly about the work rather than about the technology's effect on the world. I did not choose any source because of what it would say about which areas matter.
