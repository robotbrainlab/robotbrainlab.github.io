# Candidate research areas — the merged evidence from four framing runs

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-11-12.** Runs `a` to `d` merged: 254 distinct categories with their source counts. **Never a proposal and never comparable with a consolidated list.** This cycle's framing runs are `../2026-11-11-h.md` and `../2026-11-11-i.md`. It is input to nothing.

**This is not a proposal and nothing here is recommended.** It is every category that any
source named across four independent runs of `../../docs/briefs/TEMPLATE-area-frame.md`, with
how many sources named it and which kinds of source. **The cut is proposed by Claude and ruled on by the owner** (`../../docs/GOAL.md` §3.3) — `[2026-11-11]`, this said the cut was the owner's, which left him an inventory and no recommendation (`ROADMAP.md`
step 4). Ordered by source count, then alphabetically — the count is a fact about the evidence,
not a score and not a ranking of importance.

**254 distinct categories.** Runs: `a` and `b` each capped and self-filtered, so their
lists are superseded and kept only as record; `c` (86 areas, 12 sources) and `d` (209 areas, 15
sources) reported everything their sources named. `c` took mostly top-level categories, `d` went
a level deeper on several sources — **so these two differ in resolution, not in content.**

## What the evidence says before any cutting

1. **The academic and applied taxonomies are nearly disjoint.** Of the categories below,
   **154 are named only by review venues and indexes** and
   **49 only by benchmark suites, standards bodies and industry programmes.**
   Only **41** are named by both. A division drawn from academic sources alone omits
   the engineering half of this book by construction; one drawn from applied sources alone omits
   theory, knowledge representation, planning, multi-agent systems and probabilistic methods.
2. **The ACM classification files machine learning as a *sibling* of artificial intelligence**,
   not a child of it. Three other sources file it as one area inside AI. Both runs found this.
3. **Four incompatible axes.** The sources divide by deployment target, by engineering concern
   and audience, by method, and by a 2012 hierarchy. These are not refinements of one another.
4. **No occupational taxonomy was reachable in any run.** The job board redirected to a site
   with no categories, four times. So role-shaped categories — MLOps, research engineer, data
   engineer — are absent from all the evidence below, and their absence is the instrument's,
   not the field's.
5. **The oldest source is fourteen years old** (ACM CCS 2012), which is why it carries no
   category for deep learning, language models, generative models, evaluation or alignment.

URL and date, what it includes, what it excludes, what it overlaps, any size signal, and where
sources disagree — in `2026-11-06-c.md` or `2026-11-06-d.md`, under its own heading.
