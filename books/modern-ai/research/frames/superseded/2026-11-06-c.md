## What these sources systematically miss

- **Work that is never submitted, reviewed, indexed or advertised.** Internal industrial model
  development, deployment and incident response; proprietary training runs; evaluation done inside
  one company against its own data; everything in a frontier lab that does not become a paper, a
  benchmark submission or a conference talk. No source of any of these five kinds can see it.
- **Work with no submission category anywhere.** Prompt and context engineering, data labelling
  operations, annotation workforce management, model cost control, capacity planning, on-call for
  model services, procurement of models. S10 reaches a little of this because a programme can invent a
  track; the review venues and indexes reach none of it.
- **Classified and defence work**, which is reviewed but not publicly indexed.
- **Negative results and work that was not accepted.** Every review-venue source here lists what may
  be **submitted**, which is not the same as what exists.
- **Work whose only venue is a blog, a model card, a repository README or an internal document.** S11
  partly reaches open-source tooling, but only projects a foundation has catalogued.
- **Regional and national research systems.** No non-Anglophone classification was reached: no Chinese
  (CAAI), Japanese (JSAI), European national or Indian scheme; no journal taxonomy at all (JAIR, AIJ,
  IEEE TPAMI, JMLR); no funding-agency or patent classification (for example CPC G06N).
- **Hardware and chip design for AI**, which S9 measures but which has no subject category here;
  semiconductor venues were not consulted.
- **The one gap this run noticed and is not allowed to fill.** Several sources carry fragments of an
  "AI operations" or "serving and lifecycle" area at a level **below** the one taken (S11's "Model:
  Inference / Training / Workflow / Benchmarking"), while no source names it as a top-level category.
  This is stated here, as the brief requires, and **not added as a candidate area**.
