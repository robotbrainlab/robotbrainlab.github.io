## Disagreements between sources

1. **Is machine learning one category or six?** AAAI, arXiv, CCS and LFAI keep a single `Machine Learning`. NEURIPS and ICML have no such category and instead run `general machine learning`, `deep learning`, `theory of machine learning`, `optimization`, `probabilistic methods` and `reinforcement learning` as co-equal topics.
2. **Where machine learning sits.** CCS-AIMA shows `Machine learning` as a child of `Artificial intelligence`; CCS-UCT shows it as a sibling of `Artificial intelligence` under `Computing methodologies`. The 2012 CCS is a poly-hierarchy, so both may be true of it, but I could not open ACM's own page to check.
3. **Is machine learning inside AI at all?** arXiv says `cs.AI` covers all areas of AI *except* vision, robotics, machine learning, multiagent systems and NLP; CCS puts those inside the AI subtree (except as in 2).
4. **Does speech belong to NLP?** CCS files `Speech recognition` under `Natural language processing`. AAAI gives audio and speech its own area of 13 topics.
5. **Does probabilistic reasoning belong to knowledge representation?** CCS files `Probabilistic reasoning` under KRR. AAAI gives it a separate area, `Reasoning under Uncertainty`. NEURIPS and ICML call it `Probabilistic methods` and treat it as method, not reasoning.
6. **Search and optimization.** CCS has `Search methodologies` and no optimization node inside AI; AAAI bundles them as `Search and Optimization` and runs a second area for constraints; NEURIPS and ICML have `Optimization` and no search category.
7. **Where robotics lives.** AAAI and arXiv give robotics one category; CCS has no robotics node and scatters it across `Planning and scheduling`, `Control methods` and `Knowledge representation and reasoning`; NEURIPS bundles it with embodied systems and engineering.
8. **Ethics, trust, governance and society.** Five carvings of overlapping ground: `Philosophy and Ethics of AI` (AAAI), `trustworthy machine learning` (ICML), `Trusted & Responsible AI` plus a separate `Security & Privacy` (LFAI), `Socio-technical aspects of AI` plus `Human interaction in AI systems` (NEURIPS), and `Responsible AI` plus `Policy and Governance` (AIINDEX). Whether privacy sits inside trust is disputed between ICML (yes) and LFAI (no).
9. **Applications: one bucket or many?** AAAI and ICML keep one; NEURIPS names five (physical sciences, health and biotechnology, sustainability, social sciences, creatives); AIINDEX joins science and medicine in one chapter; arXiv has none and routes ML applications to `cs.LG`.
10. **Language models vs NLP.** NEURIPS's topic is `Language and multimodal language models` and it has no NLP topic; AAAI, arXiv, CCS and LFAI all carry NLP and file language models inside it.
11. **Evolutionary computation.** AAAI files it under `Search and Optimization`; CCS files it under `Machine learning > Machine learning approaches > Bio-inspired approaches`; arXiv has `cs.NE`, a category outside the AI ones I took.
12. **Deployment setting as a category.** MLCommons divides work into ten suites by where it runs (datacenter, edge, mobile, tiny, client, automotive, storage, HPC); no review venue or index I read divides by deployment setting at any level.
13. **MLCommons against itself.** It publishes four inference suites by setting but one combined `MLPerf Inference` working group, so its own two levels disagree on how many inference categories exist.
14. **The word "search".** It means state-space search in AAAI and CCS and information retrieval in AIEWF's `Search & Retrieval`. The sources do not disagree about a boundary here; they use one word for two categories.
15. **Agents.** AAAI files LLM agents as topics inside `Multiagent Systems`; AIEWF runs three separate agent tracks (`Agentic Engineering`, `Claws & Personal Agents`, `Agentic Commerce`); NEURIPS and ICML have no agent category.
