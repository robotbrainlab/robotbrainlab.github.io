## Which level of each source I took

- **S1 arXiv.** Levels are group → (archive, omitted for Computer Science) → category. I took
  the **category** level (cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.MA, cs.NE, cs.IR). Nothing sits
  below category.
- **S2 AAAI-26 keywords.** Two levels: 16 top-level areas, each holding keyword subtopics, about
  300 keywords in all. I took the **top-level area** level. Keyword counts per area quoted below
  are approximate hand counts made from the fetched page, against the page's own stated total of
  about 300.
- **S4 NeurIPS 2025 CFP.** Flat: one list of twelve topics, introduced as "topics including but
  not limited to the following", each with parenthetical examples. Not an exhaustive filing
  system; the examples are not a second level.
- **S5 AI Engineer World's Fair 2025.** Flat track list, grouped only by conference day; no
  sub-tracks. The page is internally inconsistent about the count: "18 tracks" in one place, "12
  tracks" in the Program Overview, and 16 numbered tracks plus 2 leadership tracks in the
  schedule, which is marked incomplete.
- **S6 MLCommons.** Two levels: card category label (AI Safety, AlgoPerf, MLPerf) → suite name
  (MLPerf Training, MLPerf Inference: Datacenter / Edge / Mobile / Tiny, MLPerf Client, MLPerf
  Storage, MLPerf Automotive, MLPerf Endpoints, MLPerf Training: HPC, AlgoPerf, AILuminate). I
  took the **suite** level. The division is by deployment tier and by stage of the system's life,
  not by subject.

## How these twelve were chosen (the cap bound)

The cap is 12. The fetched sources carry far more than 12 categories between them (16 AAAI areas,
12 NeurIPS topics, 8 relevant arXiv categories, 18 World's Fair tracks, 12 MLCommons suites), so
a selection had to be made. The rule applied, stated so it can be checked and disagreed with:

1. Keep every category named as a category, at the level taken, by **two or more** fetched
   sources: Application Domains, Computer Vision, Evaluation, Infrastructure, Intelligent Robots,
   Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, Natural Language Processing (8).
2. Fill the remaining four slots with the categories named by **one** source at the level taken
   and by the **largest number of other fetched sources at a lower level** (inside another
   category's own description): Philosophy and Ethics of AI (3 other sources), Data Mining &
   Knowledge Management (2), Planning, Routing, and Scheduling (2), Reasoning under Uncertainty
   (2).
3. Everything at one-or-fewer such mentions was dropped. The dropped set is listed in the
   coverage section. Dropping them is an artefact of this rule and the cap, not a judgement about
   the categories.

Ordering below is alphabetical.

## Disagreements between sources

1. **Is machine learning inside AI or beside it?** AAAI-26 files "Machine Learning (ML)" as one
   of 16 top-level areas of an AI conference. arXiv's cs.AI description says it "Covers all areas
   of AI except Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, and Computation and
   Language" — so cs.LG, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.MA and cs.CL are siblings of AI, not children.
   NeurIPS 2025 has no AI category at all and splits learning into "General machine learning" and
   "Deep learning".
2. **Where planning goes.** Top-level area (AAAI PRS) vs content inside cs.AI (arXiv) vs an
   example inside "Reinforcement learning" (NeurIPS).
3. **Where robotics goes.** Top-level area (AAAI ROB) vs a sibling category excluded from cs.AI
   (arXiv cs.RO) vs an example inside "Reinforcement learning" (NeurIPS) vs a deployment tier
   (MLCommons, MLPerf Automotive).
4. **Where safety, fairness and privacy go.** An ethics/philosophy area (AAAI PEAI) vs "Social
   and economic aspects of machine learning" (NeurIPS) vs inside machine learning itself (arXiv
   cs.LG lists robustness, explanation, fairness) vs outside the performance suites as its own
   benchmark category (MLCommons "AI Safety" / AILuminate) vs a security track (World's Fair).
5. **Whether uncertainty is reasoning or method.** AAAI "Reasoning under Uncertainty" vs NeurIPS
   "Probabilistic methods" vs arXiv's "uncertainty in AI" inside cs.AI.
6. **Whether evaluation is a category of work.** NeurIPS has a topic "Evaluation" and the World's
   Fair a track "Evals"; AAAI-26 has no top-level evaluation area; MLCommons, whose whole purpose
   is measurement, does not use an evaluation category — it divides by suite and deployment tier.
7. **Whether infrastructure is a category of work.** NeurIPS "Infrastructure" and World's Fair
   "AI Infrastructure" vs no such top-level category in AAAI-26 or the arXiv cs categories; and
   MLCommons divides it by deployment tier rather than keeping it whole.
8. **Retrieval.** Own category (arXiv cs.IR, which also notes overlap with cs.DL) vs inside
   data mining and knowledge management (AAAI DMKM) vs split into three tracks (World's Fair
   "Retrieval + Search", "GraphRAG", "LLM RecSys").
9. **Search vs optimisation.** AAAI has both "Search and Optimization (SO)" and "Constraint
   Satisfaction and Optimization (CSO)" as separate top-level areas; NeurIPS has a single
   "Optimization" topic and no search category; arXiv has neither at category level.
10. **Within-source inconsistencies, recorded rather than resolved.** The AAAI-26 keywords page
    says "Intelligent Robotics (ROB)" in its Submission Groups list and "Intelligent Robots
    (ROB)" in its keyword headings. The World's Fair 2025 page states 18 tracks in one place and
    12 in its Program Overview while listing 16 numbered tracks plus 2 leadership tracks.
11. **Agent engineering has no academic counterpart in these sources.** The World's Fair carries
    "MCP", "Agent Reliability" and "SWE Agents" as tracks; AAAI's agent area is "Multiagent
    Systems" and arXiv's is cs.MA, both about coordination among agents rather than the
    reliability of a deployed agent product. No fetched source merges them.
