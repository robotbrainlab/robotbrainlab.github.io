## The question

Under what categories does **`modern artificial intelligence`** currently file, review and present its
own work — and what does each category include and exclude?

### Which level of each source I took, and how each source was scoped

- **S1 arXiv.** Taken at the **category** level (the level below the "Computer Science" group; the page
  nests group → archive → category and omits the archive row for Computer Science). arXiv has no
  "artificial intelligence" super-node, so the branch was scoped by the source's own sentence under
  cs.AI — "Covers all areas of AI except Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, and
  Computation and Language" — which names its own AI siblings: cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.MA,
  cs.CL. To that I added the two further categories whose own descriptions name this subject matter,
  cs.NE and stat.ML. The archive's other ~31 cs categories (cs.HC, cs.CY, cs.IR, cs.SD, cs.SE, cs.DB …)
  are outside the branch taken and so absent here; that scoping is mine and is reported, not hidden.
  arXiv has no level below the category.
- **S2 NeurIPS.** **Flat.** The call gives one list of "topics including but not limited to the
  following" with no level above or below it.
- **S3 AAAI-27.** Taken at the **area** level (17 areas). The level above is the Main Technical Track;
  the level below splits into "over 200 topics" (per-area counts given in each unit). Authors pick one
  mandatory primary keyword from the topic level.
- **S4 IJCAI 2025.** Taken at the **track** level. The main-track call lists **no** areas at all — only
  "any aspect of artificial intelligence" — so the only named categories this venue gave are its
  special tracks and its survey track. There is no level below.
- **S5 ACM CCS 2012 via the UCT repository.** Taken at **levels 2 and 3**: level 1 is the 13 top-level
  CCS headings, level 2 the children of "Computing methodologies", level 3 the children of
  "Artificial intelligence" and of "Machine learning". Deeper CCS levels exist (e.g. "Ontology
  engineering" under KRR) and were not reached. Siblings outside the branch taken — "Computer
  graphics", "Modeling and simulation", "Concurrent computing methodologies", "Parallel computing
  methodologies" — are absent by that scoping. The scheme is a poly-hierarchy, so a concept may have
  several parents.
- **S6 MLCommons.** Taken at **level 2**, the category label above each suite (3 labels). Level 1 is the
  "Benchmark Suites" section; level 3 is the 12 named suites, which are named inside the units rather
  than reported as areas of their own.
- **S7 LF AI & Data.** Taken at the **category** level (14 categories). The level above is the landscape
  itself; the level below is subcategories, counted and named per unit. Two of the 14 classify member
  organisations rather than work; they are reported as the source names them, with that said plainly.
- **S8 AI Index.** Taken at the **chapter** level (8 chapters, numbered). Sections inside a chapter exist
  but the page does not list them, so the level below could not be read.
- **S9 AI Engineer World's Fair.** Taken at the **track** level (18 tracks). The level above is the
  conference day; individual talks sit below and were not read.

### Merges applied (and the basis for each)

1. **Natural Language Processing** ≡ arXiv **"Computation and Language" (cs.CL)**. Basis: arXiv's own
   description of cs.CL is "Covers natural language processing."
2. **Computer Vision** ≡ ACM CCS **"Computer vision"** ≡ arXiv **"Computer Vision and Pattern
   Recognition" (cs.CV)**. Basis: identical head term; arXiv's description is "Covers image
   processing, computer vision, pattern recognition, and scene understanding."
3. **Multiagent Systems** ≡ ACM CCS **"Distributed artificial intelligence"**. Basis: arXiv's own
   description of cs.MA names both in one breath — "multiagent systems, distributed artificial
   intelligence, intelligent agents, coordinated interactions."
4. **Machine learning** across S1 (cs.LG and stat.ML), S3, S5, S7. Basis: identical name.
5. **Intelligent Robotics** ≡ arXiv **"Robotics" (cs.RO)**. Basis: identical head noun.
6. **Knowledge Representation and Reasoning** across S3 and S5. Basis: identical name.
7. **Artificial intelligence** across S5 (the level-2 node) and S1 (cs.AI). Basis: identical name — but
   the two place it very differently, which is logged as a disagreement rather than resolved.
8. **Infrastructure** (S2) ≡ **"AI Infrastructure"** (S9). Basis: the names differ only by the subject
   qualifier.

No source's category was split. Where two labels were close but not plainly the same —
"Evaluation"/"Evals", "Security"/"Security & Privacy", "Responsible AI"/"Trusted & Responsible
AI"/"Philosophy and Ethics of AI", "Generative AI"/"Generative Media", "Reinforcement
Learning"/"Reasoning + RL" — they are kept as separate units with the overlap recorded.

## Disagreements between sources

1. **Where "artificial intelligence" itself sits.** S5 (ACM CCS 2012) makes *Artificial intelligence* the
   parent of computer vision, NLP, KRR, control methods, distributed AI and the philosophical
   foundations, with *Machine learning* as its **sibling** under Computing methodologies. S1 (arXiv)
   makes cs.AI a **residual** that explicitly excludes "Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent
   Systems, and Computation and Language". Same name, opposite construction.
2. **Whether machine learning is one category or six.** S3, S5, S1 and S7 keep one *Machine learning*.
   S2 (NeurIPS) splits the same ground into *General machine learning*, *Deep learning*,
   *Optimization*, *Probabilistic methods*, *Reinforcement learning* and *Theory*. S7 additionally
   splits *Deep Learning*, *Reinforcement Learning* and *Generative AI* out of it. S5 splits it
   downward instead, into *Learning paradigms* and *Machine learning approaches*.
3. **Whether vision and language are children of AI or peers of it.** S5 nests both under AI. S1 and S3
   make both peers, and S1's cs.AI names them as exclusions.
4. **Robotics.** S3 and S1 give it a category; S2 has none and names robotics as an example inside
   *Reinforcement learning*; S9 widens it to *Autonomy + Robotics*; the S5 AI children reached contain
   no robotics node.
5. **Ethics, safety, responsibility and society.** Six sources, six boundaries: S3 *Philosophy and
   Ethics of AI*; S5 *Philosophical/theoretical foundations of artificial intelligence* (no ethics);
   S2 *Social and economic aspects of machine learning*; S8 *Responsible AI* **plus** a separate
   *Policy and Governance*; S7 *Trusted & Responsible AI* (explainability, adversarial, bias and
   fairness) **plus** a separate *Security & Privacy*; S6 *AI Safety* as a benchmark label. No two
   coincide.
6. **Search, planning, constraints and optimisation.** S3 keeps three areas (*Search and
   Optimization*, *Constraint Satisfaction and Optimization*, *Planning, Routing, and Scheduling*).
   S2 keeps one (*Optimization*). S1 folds planning into cs.AI's own contents. Among the S5 AI
