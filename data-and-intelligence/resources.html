Three tracks: Mathematics (the foundational layer), Science (theoretical depth), and Engineering (practical fluency). Mathematics sits on its own because every Science topic and most Engineering work assumes it; Science and Engineering then mirror each other topic-for-topic across the same five areas.

Software Engineering
Development
Getting a model from a notebook into production. Development covers Application Engineering (writing the model code, choosing frameworks, structuring experiments) and Infrastructure Engineering (pipelines, registries, serving stack). The Operating sub-track below covers the four general engineering disciplines — Reliability, Performance, Security, Observability — that apply to any production system. Both Infrastructure Engineering and the four Operating disciplines are covered in practice by the MLOps and LLMOps spanning practices listed in the final sub-track.

MLOps & LLMOps
The two spanning practices that cover Infrastructure Engineering plus all four Operating disciplines, giving them their ML- and LLM-specific treatment. MLOps closes the model lifecycle loop — pipelines, registries, deployment, drift monitoring, retraining. LLMOps specialises the same loop for LLM-based systems — prompt and config versioning, evaluation harnesses, token-cost and latency monitoring, semantic caching, prompt-injection defense.
MLOps
The closed-loop discipline for ML systems. Foundational reference is Chip Huyen’s Designing Machine Learning Systems.
