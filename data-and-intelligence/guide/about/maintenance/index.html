1. Normal Maintenance Command#Link to this section

Read AGENTS.md and MAINTENANCE.md. Perform the maintenance action that is currently due.

On this command, the agent determines the due action from the state file and today's date, performs it, updates the state file, and reports. It does not ask the human to work out dates.

Explicit commands are also supported and may be given at any time:

Perform the 4-week Modern AI Scan.

Perform the 12-week Curriculum Review.

An explicit command runs the named action even if it is not yet due. The state file is updated from the date the action was completed.

The scan's search directions changed on 2026-11-03 (4), and the next scan must also answer the question in 12. Awareness of releases and tooling is kept outside the cadence, in 13.

2. Determining the Due Action#Link to this section

  1. Read reviews/maintenance-state.json. If it is missing or does not parse, stop. Report the problem, propose a corrected state from the most recent dated reports in reviews/ and the baseline header of ROADMAP.md (Specification), and ask the human to confirm before continuing.
  2. Compare today's date with the two due dates:
Condition Action
Today ≥ next_curriculum_review_due Perform the 12-week Curriculum Review. It includes a scan, so no separate scan is run
Otherwise, today ≥ next_modern_ai_scan_due Perform the 4-week Modern AI Scan
Neither is due Perform nothing. Report which action is next and its date
  1. Overdue actions are performed once. Do not run a catch-up series of missed scans; cover the whole period since the last action in one report.
  2. Report the action taken, the report file created, the state-file changes, and any proposals awaiting human approval.

3. Maintenance State#Link to this section

reviews/maintenance-state.json is operational metadata, not evidence. It holds:

Field Meaning
baseline The current baseline number in ROADMAP.md (Specification)
baseline_date The date that baseline was accepted
last_modern_ai_scan Date of the most recent scan, or null
last_curriculum_review Date of the most recent curriculum review
next_modern_ai_scan_due Date the next scan is due
next_curriculum_review_due Date the next curriculum review is due

Update rules. Dates are ISO YYYY-MM-DD. Intervals are counted from the date the action is completed:

After Set
A 4-week scan last_modern_ai_scan = today; next_modern_ai_scan_due = today + 28 days
A 12-week review last_curriculum_review = today; next_curriculum_review_due = today + 84 days; and last_modern_ai_scan = today; next_modern_ai_scan_due = today + 28 days
An accepted new baseline baseline and baseline_date

Compute dates arithmetically; do not estimate them.

4. 4-Week Modern AI Scan#Link to this section

In this section: Purpose and Independence · Search Directions · Anti-News-Feed Rule · Scan Procedure · Scan Report

Purpose and Independence#Link to this section

Purpose. Observe meaningful changes in contemporary AI engineering without continuously rewriting the curriculum. Cadence: every 4 weeks.

The scan is lightweight. It does not require a full independent field reconstruction (AGENTS.md — Proportional Independence). It must, however, search the world rather than the roadmap: look for what changed, then ask whether it matters.

Search Directions#Link to this section

Changed 2026-11-03. The directions used to be a list of subjects — model capability, inference and serving, retrieval, agents, context, adaptation, evaluation, security, multimodality, standards. Every one of those is a language-model subject, so a scan pointed along them could only return language-model findings, and twice it did: all 23 sources of the 2026-11-03 scan were language-model or agent papers, and the current-practice register holds no row about tabular models, time series, recommenders, media generation or embodied systems. The old list carried a note saying it was "not a closed taxonomy", and that note lost to the list both times.

So the directions now name the kind of change to look for, not the technology it happens in.

  • What can engineers now build that they could not before?
  • What changed in how systems are put together?
  • What changed in how they are measured?
  • What changed in how they fail, or are attacked?
  • What changed in what they cost, or how fast they are?
  • What became a standard, and what was dropped or deprecated?
  • What is newly disputed, and what is now settled?
  • Active research frontiers — what researchers are trying to solve, which directions have substantial evidence, which are contested, and which could reshape the field. The track carries the open problems, so the scan must look for them. Frontier findings are recorded, never promoted to practice.

Nothing in that list points at one technology, so a finding about any part of the field can come back through it. Language-model work is still found; it simply stops being the only thing the scan can see.

Two rules that go with the change.

  1. Report where practice is concentrated, as a finding in its own right. Supply no taxonomy to a searching agent and name no areas — it builds its own structure from the evidence. Never frame any part of the search as "outside language models" or against an existing category: that defines the rest of the field by reference to one part of it and sends the agent hunting for a result instead of measuring one. The distribution is a finding, not a premise.
  2. Every searching agent reports what it searched, what it could not reach, and what its sources over-represent. A claim about the field's shape is worth exactly as much as the sampling behind it.

Prefer primary evidence, as ranked in AGENTS.md — Research Standards. Record scientific evidence and engineering adoption as separate signals.

Anti-News-Feed Rule#Link to this section

The scan is not:

  • AI news summarization;
  • model-release tracking for its own sake;
  • a collection of vendor announcements;
  • a GitHub-trending digest;
  • leaderboard watching.

A development matters only if it changes something meaningful about what engineers need to understand or build, system architecture, evaluation, reliability, security, efficiency, or professional practice.

⚠️ If nothing meaningful changed, the correct result is: No curriculum-relevant change.

A short report that says so is a successful scan.

Scan Procedure#Link to this section

  1. Read the previous scan report, if any, and carry forward its open watch items.
  2. Search the period since the last scan (or since baseline_date for the first scan) along the directions above and beyond them.
  3. For each candidate, test it against the anti-news-feed rule. Discard what fails.
  4. For each survivor, decide a status on the AGENTS.md lifecycle and an action from the table below, and record it in the current-practice register with its evidence.
  5. Write the report. Update the register — and the learner guide's Modern AI: Research, Science and Engineering topics where a taught practice changed status; only Current Practice, Established and Open problem entries are taught, and the site build enforces this. Update the state file.
Action Use when
Ignore Recorded only to show it was considered
Watch Possibly important; evidence or adoption still thin
Current Practice Established enough to practise now, not yet durable curriculum
Investigate at 12-week review May require a permanent curriculum change
Superseded An existing current-practice item has been replaced
Open problem An unresolved question the track carries as a frontier, not as practice
Evidence update Corrects or refreshes evidence without changing the curriculum

What a scan may change. A scan normally does not modify the permanent curriculum. It may update the current-practice column of ROADMAP.md §6 Modern AI: Research, Science and Engineering (Specification) when the evidence is recorded in the scan report and the change does not touch the Depth Track, any block's required capability, depth or prerequisites, the durable-concept column, or mandatory resources. Any such edit must be listed in the report and in the completion message, and the site must be regenerated per AGENTS.md — Generated HTML. Everything else waits for the 12-week review.

Contents:

  1. Header — date; period covered; current baseline.
  2. Method — search directions used; sources consulted; what was deliberately excluded.

Finding Evidence Status Why it matters Action

5. 12-Week Curriculum Review#Link to this section

In this section: Purpose · Inputs · Review Procedure · Review Report

Purpose#Link to this section

Decide whether accumulated evidence requires changing the current baseline. Cadence: every 12 weeks (84 days).

Do not ask: what changed in our roadmap topics?

Ask: what does the learner need now, independent of what we currently teach?

The review applies the Independent Discovery and Falsification Rule: the independent examination comes before the comparison with ROADMAP.md (Specification).

Inputs#Link to this section

  • all scan reports since the last curriculum review;
  • fresh primary evidence, both scientific and engineering;
  • evidence of engineering adoption and current professional practice;
  • failures and limitations that have become clearer;
  • changes in maturity and durability;
  • learner feedback, if available;
  • the running notes file from 13, tested rather than accepted;
  • resource updates — new editions, withdrawn materials, better candidates — where relevant;
  • the standing maintenance items.

Scientific importance and engineering adoption are separate signals. Neither is hype, and hype is neither.

Review Procedure#Link to this section

  1. Pass 1 — Independent current-state reconstruction. From fresh evidence and the accumulated scans, write down what capabilities and knowledge a contemporary Data & Intelligence AI engineer now needs, and at what depth, before re-reading the roadmap's block lists. If the reviewing agent has already studied the roadmap in the same session, delegate Pass 1 to a fresh agent where available, or disclose the limitation in the report.
  2. Pass 2 — Comparison. Compare Pass 1 with ROADMAP.md (Specification). Classify each difference as Correct, Missing, Excessive, Misplaced, Outdated, Under-specified, Over-specified or Uncertain. Preserve conflicts; do not reconcile them silently.
  3. Resources. Apply the Resource Independence and Resource Compression rules to any proposed resource change.
  4. Bias audit. Test every item in the Permanent Bias Checklist.
  5. Proposals. Classify each proposed change and apply the Curriculum Change Rule.
  6. Write the report, update the state file, and present proposals for human approval. Make no significant change to ROADMAP.md (Specification) before approval.

Change classification.

Class Meaning
Critical The current curriculum would produce a meaningful capability defect
Important A material improvement in scope, depth, sequencing or efficiency
Optional A reasonable refinement, not needed for curriculum integrity
Evidence Update Only Corrects evidence without changing the curriculum

For each Critical or Important proposal, state: current state; finding; evidence; capability consequence; proposed change; learning-cost consequence; confidence.

Review Report#Link to this section

Location: reviews/YYYY-MM-DD-curriculum-review.md, dated on completion, following the Markdown conventions in AGENTS.md.

Sections:

  1. Executive Summary
  2. Independent Current-State Reconstruction
  3. Comparison With Current Baseline
  4. Missing Knowledge
  5. Excessive or Outdated Knowledge
  6. Misplaced Depth
  7. Dependency Changes
  8. Modern-Practice Changes
  9. Resource Changes
  10. Anti-Bloat and Compression Findings
  11. Bias Audit
  12. Proposed Changes
  13. Things Explicitly Not Changed
  14. Uncertainties
  15. Sources

Label important conclusions Observed, Inference, Judgment or Uncertain, with confidence, per AGENTS.md — Uncertainty.

7. Minor Changes#Link to this section

These may be made without an approval cycle, but must be listed in the completion message:

  • broken links;
  • corrected citation metadata;
  • typographical errors;
  • clearly superseded URLs for the same resource;
  • formatting within AGENTS.md conventions;
  • factual resource-edition metadata that does not alter what is assigned or learned.

A change is not minor if it alters what a learner must learn, to what depth, in what order, or from which assigned portions.

⚠️ Do not classify a curriculum-content change as minor to avoid approval.

8. Implementing Approved Changes#Link to this section

  1. Confirm the approved scope in the conversation. Implement only that scope.
  2. If the change is significant, create a new baseline (9): copy the current ROADMAP.md (Specification) verbatim to history/roadmap-baseline-N.md (N = the outgoing baseline) before editing.
  3. Edit ROADMAP.md (Specification). Update its baseline number, acceptance date and Maintenance Status section. Then update LEARNING-GUIDE.md to present the change; the site build fails until the guide and the curriculum correspond.
  4. Record the rationale: the curriculum-review report, plus a design/ note if the design reasoning is substantial.
  5. Update PROJECT-STATE.md and the state file.
  6. Regenerate and verify the site per AGENTS.md — Generated HTML.
  7. Verify every anchor and relative link, and verify that history/ files are unchanged.

12. Where the Dominant Practice Is — the question the next scan must answer#Link to this section

Agreed with the owner, 2026-11-03. This began as a one-off investigation. It is not one: the bias lived in the scan's standing directions, so fixing it once would have fixed nothing. The directions in 4 were changed instead, and the next scan under those directions is this investigation.

What it has to answer, alongside its normal findings:

Where is the dominant practice in AI engineering, and how is it distributed across the field?

Why it matters beyond the scan#Link to this section

It decides the scope of the second book — whether the Modern AI track has ten topics or more — and it is one of the two tasks blocking that book's first chapter. See _book/HANDOVER.md §9.5.

Two outcomes, both useful:

  • Substantial practice outside language models. The track is short of topics and the register needs rows it has never had.
  • Thin. Then "Modern AI" in this project honestly means the foundation-model layer, and ROADMAP.md §6 (Specification) should say so in one sentence rather than letting the name overclaim.

The evidence that the bias was real#Link to this section

  • The current-practice register holds 68 entries and not one concerns tabular models, time series and forecasting, recommenders, media generation or embodied systems. Multimodal input is the nearest thing, and that is still a language model.
  • All 23 sources of the 2026-11-03 scan were language-model or agent papers.
  • All three blind reconstructions run on 2026-11-03 disclosed the same limit unprompted. One wrote that its evidence was "English-language, Western, LLM-centric research. Entirely," and named non-LLM work as the largest structural gap in its report.
  • The landscape (Evidence) §A point 2 says plainly that foundation models reorganised the field without replacing it, and two of the twenty frontier questions in its §F are not about language models at all.

13. Staying Current Between Reviews#Link to this section

Agreed with the owner, 2026-11-03. The scan was being asked to do two jobs and did neither well.

The two jobs are different. Knowing that a lab shipped a tool is awareness. Deciding what the curriculum teaches is research. The Anti-News-Feed Rule bars the scan from the first, so for two cycles the scan could not report the thing the owner most wanted to know, and filled the gap with Watching rows instead. A four-week research cycle is also the wrong instrument for it: too slow to hear about a release, and too heavy to be worth running for one.

So awareness is kept separately, as a running notes file, and it is not research. Follow release notes, changelogs and the tools actually in use, as broadly as you like. No report, no register, no evidence grading, no cadence.

The one rule that makes it useful rather than noise:

Watch the tools. Write down the capability.

A product launching tells you nothing. Several products doing the same new thing tells you a capability has become expected, and that is adoption evidence. So a note reads "several vendors now sell X, so it is becoming expected practice" — never "company Y shipped Z". The product names are the evidence; the capability is the finding. This is the shape the landscape's §E already uses, where the products sit in a column headed "Implementation examples (not categories)" beside the capability they evidence.

Two consequences:

  • The notes file is an inbox, not a verdict. Things land there as they are noticed; the 12-week review is where each one is tested against 5's procedure or thrown out. It is listed among that review's inputs.
  • Expect to discard most of it. The landscape's §E says plainly that engineering importance is not scientific importance, and that several widely adopted practices carry little novelty. Most of this layer changes nothing about what an engineer must understand.
