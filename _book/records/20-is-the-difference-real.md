completeness-review: 2026-10-28. 8 must-fix, 14 worth considering. All five programs reproduced byte-exactly and the bootstrap's simulated coverage came back 95.8 per cent, so nothing was wrong with the code or the method. Four of the eight were one flaw with four faces: every time the chapter validated a claim it validated it against data engineered so the claim could not fail, then stated the conclusion without the condition. The +-0.14 came from a model where every case is a 0.70 coin, which makes run-to-run and case-sampling spread arithmetically identical - real sets are near-deterministic per case and run-to-run spread is 0.022, not 0.074. The bootstrap 'check' against ten thousand runs is an identity, because resampling a list of exactly 28 ones IS that binomial. 'Pairing removed the variation from which cases you drew' is false: that variation is all the remaining width is, and what pairing removes is the part both prompts share. And 'two hundred cases' came from a pattern pinned to its own population values; a real draw of 200 clears zero 74 times in 100. Also: trials were asked about in bank question 45 and never answered; Review Answer 4 contradicted the body; a claim about the percentile being 'an average of many' was false of the chapter's own code; and 33 agreeing cases were called 'both prompts get right' when 7 of them are both-wrong.
