It is wrong in a fixed direction and the chapter derives why: picking *k* of *n* is drawing without replacement, the product `\(\prod (n-c-i)/(n-i)\)` has every factor after the first smaller than `\((n-c)/n\)` because `\(n(n-c-i)-(n-c)(n-i) = -ci\)`, so the naive form over-states the chance of missing all the passes. The gap reaches 0.116 at 2 of 5 and k=3, and `check_sign()` verifies the inequality for every n to 12 and every c and k rather than asserting it. **(2)** A pass@k of 1.000 off a handful of trials reads as certainty and is arithmetic: once fewer than k trials failed, `comb(n-c, k)` is 0
