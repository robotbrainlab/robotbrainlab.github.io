AI Ecosystem Map

An opinionated map of the current AI/LLM engineering ecosystem for senior engineers — 22 layers, 6 meta-categories, ~150 tools. Each entry tagged for deep investment, surface awareness, rising, or overhyped. The cross-cutting opinions block at the end is the honest answer to “what would you actually run in production right now.”

Cross-Cutting Opinions

Worth Deep Investment (10+ hours)

  • Claude / GPT / Gemini APIs, MCP, vLLM, pgvector or Qdrant
  • Pydantic AI or LangGraph, Braintrust or Langfuse
  • Modal, Claude Code, LiteLLM, structured outputs, prompt caching

What’s Overhyped

  • Autonomous agent frameworks as a category — most are demoware. Write your own loop.
  • Fine-tuning for product use cases — solve with prompting + RAG + structured output first.
  • “Memory” frameworks — still unsolved; current offerings are hopeful, not robust.
  • Generic AI orchestration platforms that abstract everything — the abstractions break.
  • Devin and “AI software engineer” products — real and useful, not worth what they charge.
  • Vector DB differentiation — for 90% of teams, the retrieval pipeline matters more than the DB.
  • Guardrail vendors for non-regulated use — structured outputs + evals get you most of the way.

What’s Underhyped

  • MCP — already big, still underweighted in most roadmaps.
  • Rerankers — biggest quality lever in retrieval.
  • Document parsing quality — RAG lives and dies here.
  • Evals as a discipline — most teams have none.
  • Prompt caching — massive cost savings, low awareness.
  • Inngest / durable workflows for production agents.
  • Structured outputs as the alternative to most “agent framework” gymnastics.
  • On-device AI — small models closed the gap; the on-device stack matters strategically now.

The Honest 2026 Production Stack

Claude or GPT + LiteLLM + Pydantic + pgvector + Voyage/Cohere reranker + Braintrust or Langfuse + Modal or Bedrock + MCP + your own thin agent loop in plain Python or TypeScript.

That’s it. Everything else is optional.
