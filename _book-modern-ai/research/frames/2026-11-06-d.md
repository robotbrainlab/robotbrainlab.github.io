# How modern artificial intelligence files, reviews and presents its own work

Question asked: **Under what categories does modern artificial intelligence currently file, review and present its own work — and what does each category include and exclude?**

Run date: 2026-11-06. Everything below is taken from the category listings of the sources named; no
category here was coined by this run. The list is ordered alphabetically only. It is **not filtered**:
every category the sources name appears, including near-duplicates under different labels.

## Method and merge rule actually applied

Names are copied from the sources. Two labels were merged into one area only when they are the same
words modulo case, punctuation or plural, **or** when one source's own text equates them (arXiv's
cs.CL description says it "Covers natural language processing", so cs.CL sits under the
Natural language processing area). Labels that differ by an added or narrowing term were **left as
separate areas** (so "Robotics", "Robotics and perception", "Robotics and Control (ROBOT)",
"Robotics, embodied systems, and engineering" and "Robotics & World Models" are five entries, not one).
This is deliberately conservative: under-merging leaves the cut to the owner, over-merging would hide
what each venue actually calls things.

No source's category was split. Where a source splits what another merges, that is recorded under
`SOURCE DISAGREES` on the affected areas and in the Disagreements section of the returned report.

Scope note on ARXIV: arXiv's cs.AI entry states it "Covers all areas of AI except Vision, Robotics,
Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, and Computation and Language, which have their own subject
areas." That sentence is arXiv's own delimitation of which of its subject classes hold AI work, so the
arXiv areas reported here are exactly cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.LG, cs.MA, cs.CL, plus stat.ML (same
label as cs.LG). Other arXiv classes that in practice hold AI work (cs.NE, cs.IR, cs.HC, cs.CY, cs.SD,
eess.IV, eess.AS) are not reported as arXiv-attested areas, because arXiv's own note does not
enumerate them; this scoping is declared, not a quality filter.

## Disagreements between sources

1. **Is machine learning inside artificial intelligence?** ACM CCS 2012 places "Machine learning" as a *sibling* of "Artificial intelligence" under "Computing methodologies". arXiv places cs.LG outside cs.AI by explicit carve-out. AAAI-26 lists both as main-track topics without stating a relation. Not resolved here.
2. **Is computer vision inside artificial intelligence?** CCS files "Computer vision" as a child of the AI node. arXiv files cs.CV as a sibling class explicitly carved out of cs.AI. Same for NLP (CCS child; arXiv sibling cs.CL) and for agents (CCS "Distributed artificial intelligence" child; arXiv sibling cs.MA).
3. **Representation versus reasoning.** AAAI-26 lists "knowledge representation" and "reasoning" as two topics. ACM CCS and AAMAS each carry one combined category ("Knowledge representation and reasoning"; "Representation and Reasoning"). ACL splits reasoning again by kind, into "Discourse, Pragmatics, and Reasoning" and "Mathematical, Symbolic, Neurosymbolic, and Logical Reasoning".
4. **Search, planning, optimization, scheduling.** AAAI carries "search" and "planning" as separate topics and no scheduling category; AAMAS merges all four into "Search, Optimization, Planning, and Scheduling (SOPS)"; the three ML venues carry "optimization" alone; ICLR treats planning as an application of representation learning.
5. **Where trust-shaped work lives.** Six labels cover overlapping ground with no agreed boundary: "Responsible AI" (AIINDEX chapter), "Trustworthiness" (SC 42 WG 3), "Trustworthy machine learning" (ICML topic, naming seven concerns at once), "AI Safety" (MLCommons grouping), "AI Alignment" (AAAI track), "Ethics" (AAAI topic) — plus ICLR's "societal considerations including fairness, safety, privacy", ACL's two topics ("Ethics, Bias, and Fairness"; "Safety and Alignment in LLMs") and CVPR's three areas.
6. **One applications category or many.** ICML keeps all domains in "application-driven machine learning"; NeurIPS splits them into five "AI/ML for ..." topics; SC 42 keeps one "Use cases and applications" working group; ACL keeps a residual "NLP Applications" alongside four named domain topics.
7. **Health versus science.** The AI Index 2026 splits "Science" and "Medicine" into two chapters for the first time; NeurIPS instead joins health to biotechnology and separates the physical sciences; ICLR puts biology with the physical sciences.
8. **Deployment target as a category.** MLCommons divides inference into five suites by target (datacenter, edge, mobile, tiny, endpoints) plus client and automotive; the industry programme has one "Inference" track and one "Local AI" track; no review venue divides work this way at all.
9. **The word "search".** At AAAI and AAMAS it names a solving method; in the industry programme ("Search & Retrieval") it names document retrieval. Same word, different category.
10. **Theory's scope.** NeurIPS carries "Theory" unqualified; ICML "theory of machine learning"; ICLR "learning theory"; CVPR "Computer vision theory"; ACM CCS carries "Philosophical/theoretical foundations of artificial intelligence" instead.
11. **Explainability.** ACL 2026 carries both a standing "Interpretability and Analysis of Models for NLP" topic and a 2026 theme track on explainability, without separating them; ICLR files the same ground as "visualization or interpretation of learned representations"; ICML inside "trustworthy machine learning"; AAAI inside the alignment track as "mechanistic interpretability".
12. **SC 42's WG 2 title.** The fetched source gives "Big Data"; SC 42 meeting agendas and secondary reports give "Data". ISO's own committee page could not be read (403), so this is left unresolved.
13. **AAMAS prints two of its own area names two ways** on one page: "Representation, and Reasoning" / "Representation and Reasoning", and "Modeling and Simulation of Societies" / "Modeling and Simulation of (Artificial) Societies".
14. **Generative models.** ICLR gives them an area; ICML names them as a parenthetical inside "deep learning"; AAMAS joins them to agentic AI in one area.
15. **Agents.** ACL files them under language ("AI/LLM Agents"); AAMAS splits agent work into 11 areas; arXiv has one class (cs.MA); CCS calls the same thing "Distributed artificial intelligence"; the industry programme splits it into Agentic Engineering, Claws & Personal Agents, Computer Use and Agentic Commerce.
